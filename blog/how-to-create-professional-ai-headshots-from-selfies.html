How to Create Professional AI Headshots from Your Selfies

August 31, 2026 · 6 min read

A professional headshot used to mean booking a studio, paying a photographer, and spending an afternoon posing. AI changed that. A good AI headshot generator turns a regular selfie into a clean, polished portrait that works on a LinkedIn profile, a resume, or a company team page, and it takes minutes instead of days.

FaceBot includes headshot transformations among its AI tools, and the quality of the result depends a lot on how you prepare the source photo. This guide covers what AI headshots can do, how to create one in FaceBot, and the mistakes that ruin the result.

Why an AI headshot is worth trying

Most people do not have a recent professional photo. The one on your profile is three years old, shot on a phone at a wedding, or missing entirely. A studio session solves that but costs money and takes time, which is why so many people simply skip it.

AI headshots fix the three things that make a selfie look amateur: the background, the lighting, and the framing. The AI keeps your identity, your face stays recognizable, but the result looks like it was taken with a real camera in a controlled setting. You can also generate a small set of photos in one go, which gives you a consistent look across your profiles instead of a different random picture on every site.

Start with the right selfie

The output is only as good as the input. Follow these rules and you will avoid most retries.

Take it in natural light. Stand facing a window or go outside on a cloudy day. Light from the front softens the face. Avoid direct sun, which creates harsh shadows, and avoid backlight, which turns the face dark.

Keep the face centered and large. The AI needs to see the whole face clearly. If your face takes up less than a third of the frame, crop the photo before generating.

Use a simple background. A plain wall or an outdoor scene with nothing behind you works best. If your only photo has a cluttered background, our guide on removing and replacing photo backgrounds with AI shows how to clean it up first.

Face the camera directly. A slight angle is fine, but a strong profile view gives weaker results. Keep your head straight and your eyes open, looking at the lens.

Skip sunglasses, masks, and heavy filters. Anything that covers the face or changes its features confuses the AI and makes the headshot look off. Hats are acceptable if they do not cover the eyes.

Use a recent photo. The AI reproduces the person as they appear in the picture. A ten-year-old photo gives you a ten-year-old headshot.

How to create an AI headshot in FaceBot

Open FaceBot and import the selfie you prepared. Choose the headshot template from the styles section, pick the look you want, then tap generate. The transformation takes a few seconds, and you can preview the result immediately.

If the result does not look like you, change the source photo instead of regenerating the same one. The input is almost always the problem. When you are happy with the headshot, save it to your gallery or share it directly to the app where you need it.

Tips for realistic results

Use a photo with one face only. Group photos confuse the generator. Crop to keep just you.

Choose a neutral expression. A slight smile works everywhere. A big laugh or a serious stare narrows the places where the headshot fits.

Match the style to the use. A formal look suits LinkedIn and job applications. A more casual style works for social profiles and content. Do not use a playful transformation where people expect a professional photo.

Fix a blurry source first. If your only photo is dark or low resolution, run it through the HD enhancer before generating the headshot. Our guide on enhancing blurry photos with AI explains what the enhancer can and cannot do.

Common mistakes to avoid

Expecting a different person. An AI headshot keeps your features. It cleans the image, it does not replace your face with a model's. If the goal is a fantasy look, the selfie styles are the right tool instead.

Using a photo with closed eyes. A blink caught at the wrong moment gives a strange portrait. Pick a frame where the eyes are open and looking at the camera.

Giving up after one failure. Some photos just do not work, especially extreme angles or very low resolution. Take a new selfie facing the light and the problem almost always disappears.

Ignoring the background. Even a great face will not save a busy, distracting backdrop. Keep it simple or replace it.

Where to use your AI headshots

LinkedIn is the obvious place, but the same photo works on a resume, a company website, a speaking profile, or a newsletter author box. A consistent headshot across platforms makes you easier to recognize and looks more professional than five different pictures.

You can also generate a few variations and rotate them, or keep one set for professional use and another for social media. Once the classic headshot is done, browse our guide on AI selfie styles to try for more creative looks that share the same polished quality.

Start with the best selfie you already have, prepare it following the rules above, and you will get a headshot worth putting on every profile that matters.
