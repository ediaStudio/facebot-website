Halloween AI Photo and Video Ideas: Vampires and Zombies

October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Halloween is the one night of the year when looking scary is the whole point, and it is also the time when the photos sitting in your camera roll finally earn their keep. You do not need a costume shop or a makeup artist to meet your undead self. An AI transformation takes a selfie you already have and redraws it, fangs and all, in a few seconds.

This guide covers the spooky looks inside FaceBot, the photo rules that decide whether the result lands, and ideas to post before October 31.

The Halloween looks inside FaceBot

FaceBot transforms photos in two ways, and both have options built for this season. The first is a photo style. Import a picture of your face and the app redraws it as a character. Two of the styles are made for Halloween: Vampire turns you pale, dark-eyed, and sharp-fanged, while Zombie goes for cracked skin and a hollow stare. Like the other styles in the app, both keep your features, which is exactly why they work. The person in the picture is clearly you, only undead.

The second is a video effect. FaceBot's video side animates the same photo, and the list includes a Zombie clip and a Vampire Reveal. There is also an Age Progression effect that fast-forwards a face by decades, which fits the season better than you would expect: meeting the oldest version of yourself is a classic October scare.

Both looks live in the app all year, but October is when everyone wants them. If you see friends posting vampire versions of themselves this month, you now know where they made them.

Start with a photo that can take the transformation

Halloween is not famous for good lighting, and the photos people actually want to use are often the blurriest ones on the phone. A few simple rules will save you most of the retries.

Pick a recent, sharp photo. The face should be in focus and take up a good part of the frame. Choose something recent: the transformation builds on what the camera actually saw.

Keep one face in the picture. The style is applied to a single face, so crop out friends before you start, even if the group photo is your favorite.

Light the face from the front. Window light or a well-lit room beats a dramatic side shadow. The vampire look has to see your skin and your eyes clearly to repaint them convincingly.

Skip sunglasses and heavy filters. Anything that hides the face or bends its features works against you, however good the original looks.

Rescue dark or soft photos first. If your best shot is blurry or dull, clean it up with the FaceBot HD enhancer before the transformation. Our guide on enhancing blurry photos with AI explains what the enhancer fixes and what it cannot.

How to make your vampire or zombie photo

Open FaceBot and import the photo you prepared. Browse the styles until you find the Vampire and Zombie cards, pick one, and tap generate. The app redraws your face in a few seconds, and the portrait is yours to save, set as a profile picture for the season, or drop into a story.

Generate both looks while you are there, because they read very differently on the same face. Vampire is the polished option, the movie poster one. Zombie is the prank option, the one you send to the family group chat without a warning. Small changes in the source photo, a different angle or expression, shift the mood more than the style itself, so keep the version where you still recognize yourself.

One practical note: photo styles cost fewer credits than video generations, so testing several looks is cheap. Your balance sits in the top corner of the screen.

Bring the transformation to life

A still image is a good start, but movement is what people stop to watch. FaceBot's video effects animate your photo, so instead of a frozen vampire you get a short clip with real motion: a turn of the head, a blink, the monster settling into character. The Zombie and Vampire Reveal effects cover the classic monsters, and the same photo can come back as an Age Progression clip for a slower, deeper scare.

Video generations take longer than photo ones, usually under a minute, and you can leave the screen while they run: FaceBot notifies you when the clip is ready. The result arrives in vertical format, ready for TikTok, Reels, or a story. Our guide on making AI videos from your photos covers the wider video workflow.

Ideas worth trying before October 31

The haunted ancestor. Old family photos turn out even better than selfies for this season. A grandparent's portrait that blinks and turns its head is the politest haunting most families will ever experience. Digitize the photo, clean it up, and animate it in FaceBot, and the reaction around the table is worth it.

The costume audition. If you are still deciding what to wear, generate a few looks first and let the AI do the fitting. A vampire, a zombie, an ancient version of yourself. It is faster than a costume rental, and you will know which idea suits your face before you commit.

The before and after. Post your original selfie next to the transformed version in one collage. The contrast is the joke, and people stop scrolling to compare the two faces.

The countdown. The week before Halloween, post one clip per day: a zombie on Monday, the age progression on Tuesday, a vampire on Wednesday. Each piece takes minutes to make, and the small series gives people a reason to come back.

Get your spooky clips seen

Halloween content starts with an advantage: everyone is already in the mood for strange things, so your clip competes mostly with itself. Keep the format simple. Shoot vertical, keep it short, and make the transformation visible in the first second. Add a trending sound before you post and let the season do the rest. Our guide on creating viral AI content for TikTok and Reels covers the formats that tend to perform.

Common mistakes to avoid

Starting from a dark photo. The enhancer can fix softness and low light, but it cannot invent a face the camera never captured. Take a new selfie facing a window instead of pushing a bad one through the vampire style.

Expecting a different person. The styles keep your features, so if the result does not look like you, change the source photo instead of regenerating the same one.

Using someone else's face. If you want to zombify a friend or a partner, ask first and keep it friendly. The fun depends on the person recognizing themselves and laughing about it.

Waiting for the night itself. Post during the week before Halloween, while people are still hunting for ideas and costumes. By the 31st, every feed is already full of monsters.

Pick your best selfie, decide between pale and rotten, and let the app do the rest. What is genuinely scary is how little effort it takes.
